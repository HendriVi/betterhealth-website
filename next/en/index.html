Approach

Four layers, one picture.

Stress is not a character trait. It is a process in which perception, body, behaviour and environment take part at the same time. We look at them together because each one changes the others.

  1. 01

    Perception & expectation

    The brain constantly predicts what danger, effort and safety mean. These predictions shape how heavy a day feels.

    We look at Expectations · Interpretation · Attention

  2. 02

    Body & biochemistry

    Thoughts and expectations trigger bodily reactions. In turn, sleep, hormones, metabolism, inflammation and the autonomic nervous system colour what we perceive.

    We look at Sleep · Hormones · Metabolism · Nervous system

  3. 03

    Behaviour & recovery

    What we do, what we avoid and how we recover keeps a state going or releases it.

    We look at Habits · Avoidance · Recovery · Movement

  4. 04

    Environment & load

    Responsibility, workload and relationships decide how much adaptation the body has to sustain (allostatic load).

    We look at Work · Responsibility · Relationships · Rhythm

This is our understanding, not proof in an individual case. Scientific theories help us formulate questions. Clinical evidence, your circumstances and professional judgment decide which steps are justified.

Recognition

Why we start here

We start from your description, not from a diagnosis.

When it does not add up.

  1. 01

    You are functioning, and yet everything costs more effort than it used to.

    The question behind it What keeps your system on constant alert, and what would relieve it?

  2. 02

    You sleep, but you do not recover.

    The question behind it What disturbs recovery: thoughts, body, rhythm or environment?

  3. 03

    The tension stays, even when nothing acute is happening.

    The question behind it Which expectations does your brain treat as dangerous, and since when?

  4. 04

    You are told it is “just stress”. You sense it is more.

    The question behind it What exactly is happening in your case, and how sure can we be?

These examples do not replace a diagnosis.
