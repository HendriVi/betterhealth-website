## 4. Art-direction analysis

- **[V]** One typeface, one accent colour, uppercase 800-weight headings, rounded pill buttons. Nothing on the page is decorative apart from the photographs.
- **[I]** The saturated purple and heavy uppercase type give the page the look of a media brand or a course platform, built to be recognised in a social feed. Every element repeats one button style, so the visitor always knows where to click.
- **[I]** Placing the founders as large cut-outs flanking the headline puts the people first; the offer is presented as "learn from the team".
- **[I]** The page is deliberately short: one stat row, one lead magnet, four products, one FAQ, one bio. There is no navigation depth on the page itself beyond the header.
- **[V]** The long legal disclaimer in the footer is in small type on purple, below the call to action.

## 5. Copywriting analysis

Verified copy:
- "DO YOU WANT TO SCALE YOUR BUSINESS? Learn from the team that has scaled a portfolio to over $250m+ in annual revenue." (a yes/no question, then credentials).
- Stat row labels: "BUSINESSES ADVISED / NPS / CLIENT RETENTION / WORKSHOP PARTICIPANTS".
- "FREE ADVANCED TRAINING — Videos, eBooks, Guides, Templates, Downloads & more to help you succeed."
- FAQ, workshop: "**It's not an event. It's a workshop. At my office.** Expect zero motivational talks. Meaning - we get tactical. On day one, we break down how we fast track growth, recruit top talent, and frameworks we use for 'high return' decision making. This is the same stuff we use in our portfolio. On day two, we get hands on…"
- FAQ, qualification: "To register… we require you to meet minimum requirements. Our Workshop Sales Team would love to talk to you about those requirements." "We require you to meet some minimum entry requirements to be allowed in the room. These requirements include but are not limited to being active in business, a revenue threshold and operating in an industry where we are confident we can provide value."
- Founders: "culture of praise over punishment"; "audience of over 12M+".
- Footer disclaimer: "Alex and Leila Hormozi's results are not typical and are not a guarantee of your success…"

Analysis:
- **[I] Awareness stage (Schwartz):** the visitor is assumed to be problem-aware ("scale") and the offer a known category; the page does not explain what scaling is. It opens with a question that qualifies by ambition.
- **[I] Value stack (Hormozi lens):** free assets (courses, roadmap, books) establish reciprocity; the paid event is described by two days with concrete content (frameworks day one, small groups with directors in marketing, sales, people, profit and strategy on day two) and by what it is not ("not an event… zero motivational talks").
- **[I] Differentiation (Dunford):** "It's not an event" positions against conference-style business education; the contrast is stated in two short sentences.
- **[V] Qualification as copy:** the page tells the reader exactly how they will be screened (active business, revenue threshold, industry fit) and that they will speak with a "concierge team". **[I]** This treats selectivity as part of the value.
- **[V] Numbers:** 10,000, 81, 97.1 %, 12,000, $250m+, $46.2M, 1M copies, 12M+ audience. None carries a definition, date or source on the captured page; "client retention 97.1 %" does not say retention of what product. **[U]**
- **[V] Contradiction-checking:** the revenue figure differs between the home page ($250m+) and the books page ($200,000,000) (§1).
- **[V] Voice:** first-person singular in FAQ ("at my office"), short sentences, blunt, spoken. The hero headline is a question; the CTA is first person ("I'M READY TO SCALE"; "GET MY CUSTOM SCALING ROADMAP").
- **[V] CTA wording:** first-person, outcome-labelled buttons; "Take This Course" for free items, "Attend a Workshop" for the paid one; secondary "Click here" inside the FAQ to start qualification.

## 8. Distinctive mechanisms worth investigating further

1. **Qualification stated openly** ("minimum entry requirements… a revenue threshold").
2. **"It's not an event. It's a workshop."** negative-definition positioning.
3. **A short page with one visual style** for every action.
4. **A stat row** (4 numbers with small uppercase labels) directly under the hero.
5. **Free assets as the main acquisition route** (labelled "It's Free").
6. **The founders as the hero image.**
7. **A first-person CTA** ("I'm ready to scale").
8. **A legal disclaimer on non-typical results** placed on the page.

## 9. Transferable principles for BetterHealth

(All **[H]**.)
- Say plainly who the practice is for and not for, and what the first conversation will establish; this is selection that serves patients, not pressure.
- Define the service by what it is not when that helps ("not a 15-minute medication check", "not a lab panel without interpretation"), in plain and truthful terms.
- Put one visible, concrete figure row near the top only if each figure can be defined and sourced (for BetterHealth, e.g. length of first assessment, number of clinicians, response time: operational facts, not outcome claims).
- Offer useful free material (a short guide, a written explanation of the assessment) before the enquiry, but do not make it a gate.
- Keep one button style and one accent for actions.
- Place a clear statement of the limits of what can be promised close to the call to action, as Acquisition does with its disclaimer.

## 10. Limitations, open questions, misapplication risks

- **Limits:** only two pages captured; the workshop, roadmap and course pages (where the conversion actually occurs) are missing; no price seen; no REA run; the HubSpot forms and any pop-ups not tested; accordion not opened in the capture; hover states not captured.
- **Open questions:** What does the workshop cost? What does the roadmap ask for? What is the conversion path from free course to call? How are "81 NPS" and "97.1 % retention" defined?
- **Misapplication risks for BetterHealth:**
  - Direct-response tactics (urgency, "Brand new", all-caps, revenue boasts) are suited to business education sold to ambitious founders. In healthcare they could read as pressure on a vulnerable person and may breach advertising rules.
  - "Revenue" and "results" claims have no equivalent that BetterHealth can state; patient outcomes are confidential and regulated.
  - The founders-as-hero device depends on a large audience (12M+); a small practice has not earned the same recognition, and a clinician's face must be shown for different reasons (identity and accountability).
  - Heavy uppercase type, saturated purple and pill-button repetition would conflict with a calm clinical tone.
  - The "get the right fit" screening is only appropriate if BetterHealth is actually able to refuse or refer patients, and it must be framed as clinical suitability.
