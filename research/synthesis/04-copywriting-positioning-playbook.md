# Copywriting and positioning playbook for BetterHealth

**Basis:** the 12 dossiers (`../dossiers/`) and the comparative report (`02-…`). Principles are mechanisms observed on competitor sites, restated as rules; none is shown to raise conversion. Every example is **original** and may not reproduce competitor wording.
**Facts I do not have:** BetterHealth's fees, the clinician's name and qualifications, session lengths, testing offered, response times, outcome data. These appear as `[placeholders]`. Nothing may be published until the practice fills them with true statements.
**Language:** examples are in English; German drafts (§9) need review by a native speaker with medical-writing experience.
**Not legal advice:** §10 lists points to verify with counsel.

## 1. Positioning (proposal for the owner to confirm)

Structured with April Dunford's components. The text is a **hypothesis**; confirm each line against what the practice actually does.

| Component | Draft |
|---|---|
| **For whom** | Adults who pay privately for their care and live with persistent mental, cognitive or stress-related symptoms (for example exhaustion, poor concentration, anxiety, low mood, sleep that does not restore) that existing treatment has not fully explained. |
| **Competitive alternative** | A short standard psychiatric appointment focused on a diagnosis and a prescription; a general health check or lab panel without psychiatric interpretation; wellness programmes. |
| **Unique attribute** | A physician-led, written clinical formulation that brings together psychiatric history, brain function, physiology and daily context; tests are chosen by clinical question, not as a package; the plan is reviewed against agreed measures. |
| **Value** | A clearer explanation of what is going on, a plan whose steps have reasons, and a way to see whether it is working. |
| **Context** | Private, confidential, self-pay practice in Aarau; one responsible physician. |

**Why this and not "root cause" (Parsley, Chenot) or "optimise" (Wild Health):** the research shows these words attract attention but promise a single hidden cause or an upgrade. For psychiatric presentations the causes are usually several and uncertain. The positioning promises **explanation and a reviewed plan**, which the practice can deliver and show.

## 2. Message hierarchy

1. **Promise (one sentence):** a clearer explanation for symptoms that have not made sense, and a plan that is checked.
2. **Three supports:** (a) *Understanding first*: a structured assessment that reasons about the whole picture; (b) *Proportionate*: tests and treatments chosen for a stated reason; (c) *Reviewed*: progress measured and the plan revised.
3. **Proof the practice can give:** the process (steps, duration), the physician (named, qualified, responsible), the fee facts, the confidentiality practices, and how progress is tracked.
4. **Action:** a confidential conversation, with what happens next stated.

## 3. Voice

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| Second person, present tense, concrete nouns | "We transform", "unlock", "optimise your potential" |
| Sentences ≤ 22 words; one idea each | Stacked abstract nouns ("holistic, transformative, personalised care") |
| State limits ("We do not treat emergencies.") | Absolute claims ("most comprehensive", "world-class", "cure", "reverse") |
| Name what the patient does and receives | Describe equipment as the benefit |
| Say "may", "often", "in our experience" where evidence is uncertain | "Root cause", "detox", "proven" without a cited source |
| Use the patient's words for symptoms | Disease labels as the opening line |

**Word lists**
- *Prefer:* explain, make sense of, work out, what contributes, formulation (explained once), proportionate, review, measure, agree, confidential, responsible physician.
- *Avoid:* cure, reverse, detox, root cause, biohack, optimise, peak, elite, revolutionary, breakthrough, miracle, guaranteed, world-leading, cutting-edge, transform, unlock, journey (as a metaphor for care).

## 4. Copy mechanisms, with original examples

Each row: the mechanism, where it was observed, the principle, and an example for BetterHealth.

### 4.1 Recognition (patient words, then the clinical question)
*Observed:* Parsley's symptom lists in patient words; Heartwood's "behind every problem is a pattern".
*Principle:* open with the experience the reader would recognise, then say what question it raises for a clinician.
*Example:* "You sleep, but you do not feel rested. You can work, but concentrating costs more than it used to. Each doctor has an explanation for one part. **We ask how the parts connect.**"

### 4.2 Reframe without a causal promise
*Observed:* Parsley ("why — not just what") and Chenot ("root cause"). *Avoid the promise.*
*Principle:* shift from label to explanation, and say that explanations are often plural.
*Example:* "A diagnosis tells you what to call the problem. It rarely tells you why it is happening to you now. We work out which factors are likely to contribute, say how sure we are, and plan around that."

### 4.3 Mechanism: the process as numbered steps (with a deliverable)
*Observed:* Biograph (three steps with a mock-up), Function (four steps), Spring (five steps with times).
*Principle:* five short steps, each with what the patient does and receives; include the review step.
*Example:*
1. **Talk.** A first conversation, `[duration]`, with `[Dr. Name]`. You describe what is happening in your own words.
2. **Gather.** We review your records and, where it answers a clinical question, arrange specific tests. We tell you why each is needed.
3. **Formulate.** You receive a written summary: what we think is contributing, how confident we are, and what is still unknown.
4. **Plan.** We agree the first steps with you, in order of likely benefit and of risk.
5. **Review.** At `[interval]` we check progress against measures we agreed with you, and adjust.

### 4.4 Offer and fee, itemised
*Observed:* Kusnacht (inclusions list, exclusions, "paid directly by the client"), Parsley (credit toward membership, commitment and cancellation stated), Biograph.
*Principle:* name, purpose, fee with unit, duration, inclusions, exclusions, terms.
*Example:* "**First assessment.** `[CHF ___]`, `[__] minutes` with the physician plus `[time]` of preparation and a written summary. Includes: review of your records; formulation summary; a plan for the next three months. Does not include: laboratory or imaging costs, which we quote before ordering; ongoing treatment. `[If true:]` If you continue with us within `[__]` days, `[CHF ___]` is credited."

### 4.5 Suitability, openly stated
*Observed:* Acquisition.com states entry requirements; none observed states clinical suitability.
*Principle:* say who the practice is for, who it is not for, and what happens then.
*Example:* "This practice may be right if you want a thorough explanation and are willing to work on a plan. It is not an emergency service. If you are in crisis, call `[emergency number]`. If we are not the right place for your question, we will say so and, where possible, suggest who is."

### 4.6 Confidentiality, in operational terms
*Observed:* Lanserhof's AI page (what is collected, where processed, how long kept, health information optional); Kusnacht ("no exterior signage", "anonymous from first contact").
*Principle:* every confidentiality sentence states a practice, not a feeling.
*Example:* "You do not need to describe your symptoms to contact us. Your message is read only by `[who]`. We do not share it with anyone outside the practice without your consent, except where the law requires. `[Hosting location; retention period.]`"

### 4.7 The physician, accountable
*Observed:* Wild Health and Function name clinicians; Spring and Biograph show institutions more than people.
*Principle:* name the responsible doctor, list qualifications plainly, say what they do in the process.
*Example:* "**`[Dr. Name]`**, `[specialist title]`. Responsible for your assessment and plan from the first conversation to every review. `[Qualifications, registration, languages.]`"

### 4.8 Evidence and its limits
*Observed:* Chenot (study summary of biomarkers, full text gated); Wild/Parsley/Spring (percentages without method).
*Principle:* when citing research, give authors, year, what was measured, and the size and limits of the finding; link the source. When citing the practice's own data, give n, period and instrument.
*Example (research):* "A `[year]` study of `[n]` adults with `[condition]` found `[finding]`. It measured `[outcome]`, not `[what it did not measure]`. `[Source link]`." 
*Example (no own data yet):* "We do not publish outcome percentages because we have not yet collected enough data to do so responsibly. Here is how we track progress with each patient: `[scales]`, at `[intervals]`."

### 4.9 Objection handling (FAQ questions that must be answered)
1. What happens if you find something serious? *(Biograph, Kusnacht)*
2. I am asking for someone else. How do I raise this with them? *(Kusnacht)*
3. Does this replace my current doctor or therapist? *(Biograph, Parsley)*
4. Why is it self-pay and what does it cost over three months?
5. Will anyone find out I contacted you?
6. Do I need tests, and who decides?
7. How long until I hear back?
8. What if I have been told it is "just stress"?
9. Can I bring my existing records and results?
10. What do you not treat?

### 4.10 Call-to-action wording

| Moment | Label | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| Primary (all pages) | **Request a confidential conversation** | Names what the visitor gets and that it is private |
| Secondary | See how a first visit works | Leads to §4.3 |
| After fee block | Ask about the first assessment | |
| On forms | Send request | Not "Submit" |
| Phone | Call `[number]` | Number visible; hours stated |
| Avoid | "Book now", "Get started", "Transform", "Limited spots" | Urgency and generic verbs |

**Form microcopy**
- Above the button: "What happens next: `[Name]` reads your message within `[time]` and contacts you by your chosen method. You do not need to describe your symptoms."
- Error: "Please add your first name." / "Please add an email address or a phone number."
- Confirmation: "Thank you. We have your request. You will hear from us by `[day/time]`. If it is urgent, call `[number]`; in an emergency call `[emergency number]`."

## 5. Claims ladder (what may be said, in order of safety)

1. **Process facts:** steps, durations, who sees you. *Allowed when true.*
2. **Credentials and responsibilities:** degrees, titles, registration, memberships. *Allowed when verifiable.*
3. **Fee facts:** amount, unit, inclusions, exclusions, cancellation. *Allowed.*
4. **Confidentiality practices:** operational statements. *Allowed when implemented.*
5. **Research statements:** with citation and limits. *Allowed with full citation.*
6. **Own outcome data:** only with n, period, instrument, selection rule; no percentages without them. *Only when collected.*
7. **Promises of cure, reversal, root cause, guaranteed results, comparative superlatives, testimonials of patients, celebrity endorsements, invented awards.** *Never.*

## 6. Homepage copy skeleton (direction-neutral)

Section order follows the visual specification's components. Word budgets in brackets.

1. **Hero (C2)** [headline ≤ 14 words; sub ≤ 28]
   - H1: "A clearer explanation for symptoms that haven't made sense." *(the existing site's hero idea, retained)*
   - Sub: "Physician-led assessment in Aarau for adults whose exhaustion, concentration, anxiety or low mood has not been fully explained."
   - Buttons: **Request a confidential conversation** · See how a first visit works
   - Fact strip: `[__] minutes` first visit · One responsible physician · Reply within `[time]` · Fee set before we begin.
2. **Recognition (C4)** [~110 words]: 4 entries as in §4.1.
3. **Reframe + diagram (C11)** [~80 words + diagram]: §4.2; the diagram shows how history, brain function, physiology and daily context are considered together, with the sentence: "We look at these together because each can affect the others."
4. **Process (C5)** [~170 words]: §4.3.
5. **Concerns we assess (C3 + list)** [~120 words]: 5–6 concerns, each one line naming the clinical question (for example, "Sleep that does not restore: what is disturbing it, and is treatment targeted at the right thing?").
6. **Physician (C7)** [~90 words]: §4.7.
7. **Fees and first assessment (C6)** [~110 words]: §4.4.
8. **How we work with your other doctors / limits (C3)** [~80 words]: §4.5, plus continuity of care and when we refer on.
9. **Confidentiality (C3)** [~80 words]: §4.6.
10. **FAQ (C9)** [8–12 items]: §4.9.
11. **Final action (C10)** [~60 words + form]: "Start with a conversation. You do not need to explain everything now."

## 7. Page briefs

| Page | Job | Must contain |
|---|---|---|
| Approach | Show the reasoning | Formulation explained with a fictional example; tests chosen by question; how plans are reviewed; limits of the evidence |
| Care / Concerns | Let visitors find themselves | One section per concern: patient-word description; the questions we ask; what assessment includes; what we do not do |
| First visit and fees | Remove uncertainty | Offer card, timeline, preparation list, what to bring, what happens if we continue or not |
| About | Establish accountability | Physician block, practice values stated as practices, location, languages |
| Insights | Show thinking | 6–10 short essays (600–900 words) with citations; each ends with the enquiry action |
| Contact | Lower the first step | Telephone, secure message, form (4 inputs), what happens next, emergency note |
| Privacy | Be specific | Data collected, location, retention, who reads, rights |

## 8. Copy tests to run once the site is live (hypotheses)

Primary measures: **qualified enquiry rate**, completed enquiry rate, suitability of enquiries, consult-to-patient conversion. Clicks alone are not enough. Medical advertising and privacy obligations come first.

1. Fact strip above the fold vs. none: does it change enquiry completion?
2. Fee shown on the homepage vs. only on the first-visit page.
3. Recognition list in patient words vs. a headline about method.
4. Two-field form (name + one contact) vs. four fields.
5. "Request a confidential conversation" vs. "Send a message".
6. Process steps with a fictional deliverable vs. text only.

Run one test at a time; set the minimum sample in advance; with low traffic, treat results as directional and complement them with 5–8 moderated sessions with people who resemble prospective patients.

## 10. Points to verify with counsel and the practice (not legal advice)

- Swiss federal and cantonal rules on advertising by physicians (objectivity, no misleading claims, no inducements); professional-conduct rules on testimonials and awards.
- Professional secrecy and patient-data handling under the revised Swiss Federal Act on Data Protection (nFADP); where forms, email and messaging apps are hosted; whether messaging apps may be used for first contact (the Kusnacht and Chenot pattern); retention periods.
- Use of third-party scripts, fonts and analytics on a medical site; cookie consent wording.
- Any use of an AI assistant (the Lanserhof pattern) requires separate clinical-safety and data-protection review; it is **not** part of this proposal.
- Fee transparency obligations towards self-paying patients; tariff and invoicing terms.
- Claims about research, tests and treatments: each needs a source the practice can defend.
