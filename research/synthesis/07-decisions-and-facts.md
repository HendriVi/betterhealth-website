## 3. Fee reasoning (and its limits)

**Owner's instruction:** first assessment in the range CHF 500–1,000; choose the highest figure that is still within the same class; must not look cheap or commoditised; look at how Alex Hormozi prices.

**Market evidence found (weak, secondary):**
- A 2025 guide to therapy costs in Zurich gives private sessions of **CHF 180–253** per 50-minute session and a psychiatrist visit of **CHF 212** per hour (insured patients): [therapyroute.com](https://www.therapyroute.com/article/how-much-does-therapy-cost-in-zurich-complete-2025-guide).
- A directory-style 2026 guide says private practices without insurance recognition "often" charge **CHF 200–350 per hour**: [nume.ch](https://www.nume.ch/psychologen-schweiz-2026-adressen-preise-experten-tipps/). A Zurich psychiatrist listing on Psychology Today shows CHF 200 per session for self-payers.
- **No published price** was found for a comparable Swiss "stress/burnout assessment" or executive health check; Swiss Medical Network describes such a programme (including a cortisol profile, HRV, sleep screening and burnout-risk assessment) without a fee: [swissmedical.net](https://www.swissmedical.net/en/check-up/check-up-for-executives). Price would have to be requested from clinics.
- **Limits:** these are directory and guide pages, not tariff sheets; some figures conflict. I have **no evidence** for what the top of the market charges for this specific niche. The statement "CHF 1,000 is at the upper end" rests on the hourly rates above and a rough estimate of clinician time.

**Reasoning for CHF 1'000 (a judgment):** hourly private rates of about CHF 200–350 and a first assessment that takes roughly 90 minutes plus preparation and a written formulation (about 3–4 clinician hours) put the cost basis near CHF 700–1,200. CHF 1'000 is the top of the owner's range, inside that band, and signals a premium service without sitting far outside the market. **CONFIRM** against the real time spent per case.

**Hormozi's pricing logic (from secondary summaries of *$100M Offers*; I did not read the book):** price on the value of the outcome, not on delivery cost; choose the "high-value leader" position rather than the cheapest; a higher price filters for committed clients; value rises with a bigger desired outcome and a higher perceived likelihood, and falls with delay and effort (the "value equation"). Sources: [supersummary](https://www.supersummary.com/100m-offers/summary/), [ecomcrew](https://www.ecomcrew.com/100m-offers-book-summary/).

**What is applied and what is deliberately not:**

| Hormozi-style tactic | Applied here? | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Price on value, not cost; no discounting; high-value position | **Yes** | fits the "not cheap" instruction |
| Make the price filter for fit; state who it is for | **Yes** (suitability section) | |
| Raise perceived likelihood and cut delay and effort | **Yes, in clinical terms**: a written formulation, a plan, a defined next step, a reply within 24–48 h, a first assessment that does not need prior testing | |
| Bonuses, stacked extras, urgency, scarcity, guarantees of results | **No** | in a clinical service these are misleading or pressure a vulnerable person, and conflict with the playbook's claims ladder; Swiss professional advertising rules are likely to restrict them (**verify with counsel**) |
| Price anchoring against a "value" figure | **No** | unsourced anchors (see Function's "$15,000") cannot be defended |
| Selling the ongoing programme at a fixed package price on the page | **Not yet** | owner says case-dependent; the site promises a written fixed-fee proposal after the assessment |

## 4. Positioning update (supersedes playbook §1 where they differ)

The owner's description: BetterHealth is **not a psychiatric practice per se**. It applies knowledge from psychiatry, with influences from philosophy, neuroscience and biochemistry, to people living with **stress in demanding environments** and stress-related difficulties such as **anxiety and low mood**. Its approach is built on the **stress response** (allostasis), on the view that **a thought is itself a physiological event and mind and body cannot be separated**, on **systems thinking**, and on **predictive processing**. It does **not** work with **borderline personality disorder, schizophrenia or schizoaffective disorder** ("pharmaceutically informed psychiatry").

Site wording follows this. It does **not** claim "psychiatric practice", "precision psychiatry" or "novel treatment". "Novel" and "predictive processing" are not shown as proven facts; they are described as the practice's understanding.
